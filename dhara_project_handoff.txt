## Chapter 1. Theory — Convection Module

### 1.1 Governing Equations

The convection module (problem = 'convection') solves the incompressible
Navier–Stokes equations coupled with the transport of a scalar field phi
(e.g. a tracer or temperature field with no buoyancy feedback). With
velocity u and pressure p:

    div(u) = 0

    d(u)/dt + (u . grad)u = -grad(p) + (1/Re) * laplacian(u)

    d(phi)/dt + (u . grad)phi = (1/(Re*Pr)) * laplacian(phi)

Pressure enforces the incompressibility constraint (solved via a
projection/Poisson step); the scalar phi is transported passively — it does
not feed back into the momentum equation. This is the key distinction from
the Rayleigh–Bénard module (Chapter 3), where an analogous scalar
(temperature) DOES couple back into the momentum equation through a
buoyancy term.

### 1.2 Non-Dimensionalization and Governing Parameters

Velocity, length, and time are non-dimensionalized by a reference velocity,
length, and their ratio. The resulting governing (dimensionless) parameters
are:

| Parameter | Meaning                                                    |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Re        | Reynolds number — ratio of inertial to viscous forces        |
| Pr        | Prandtl number — ratio of momentum to scalar diffusivity     |

### 1.3 Closure Relations and Boundary Conditions

- Pressure is recovered by solving a Poisson equation from the divergence of
  the provisional velocity field at each sub-step.
- No additional equation-of-state closure is required (unlike the
  compressible module), since the flow is treated as incompressible.
- Standard boundary options are periodic, no-slip/no-penetration wall, and
  Dirichlet/Neumann conditions on phi, selected per sub-problem.

### 1.4 Relationship to the Rayleigh–Bénard Module

The convection module can be viewed as the Rayleigh–Bénard module
(Chapter 3) with the buoyancy coupling term switched off — i.e. the scalar
field is carried by the flow but does not drive it. This makes it useful
both as a standalone passive-scalar transport solver and as a
numerical/verification baseline for the Rayleigh–Bénard module.

## Chapter 2. Theory — Quantum Module

### 2.1 Governing Equation

The quantum module (problem = 'quantum') solves the time-dependent
Schrödinger equation for a complex wavefunction psi(x, t) in a prescribed
external potential V(x):

    i*hbar * d(psi)/dt = -(hbar^2 / 2m) * laplacian(psi) + V(x) * psi

### 2.2 Non-Dimensionalization and Governing Parameters

Lengths, times, and energies are non-dimensionalized using a reference
length L, mass m, and hbar, giving the non-dimensional evolution equation:

    i * d(psi)/dt = -(1/2) * laplacian(psi) + V_tilde(x) * psi

| Parameter | Meaning                                                          |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| V_type    | Form of the external potential (e.g. harmonic, well, barrier)      |
| g         | Nonlinear interaction strength (§2.4; zero for the linear case)    |

### 2.3 Potential Terms and Normalization

The wavefunction is normalized so that the integral of |psi|^2 over all
space equals 1, interpreted as a probability density. Supported built-in
potentials include harmonic-oscillator wells, finite/infinite square wells,
and barrier/step potentials for tunnelling test cases; custom potentials can
be supplied as a grid-defined array.

### 2.4 Linear vs. Nonlinear (Gross–Pitaevskii) Mode

Setting a nonzero interaction strength g adds a cubic nonlinear term,
switching the solver from the linear Schrödinger equation to the
Gross–Pitaevskii equation, used for mean-field modelling of Bose–Einstein
condensates:

    i * d(psi)/dt = -(1/2) * laplacian(psi) + V_tilde(x) * psi + g * |psi|^2 * psi

With g = 0 the solver reduces to the standard linear Schrödinger equation of
§2.1–2.2.

## Chapter 3. Theory — Rayleigh–Bénard Convection Module

### 3.1 Governing Equations

The Rayleigh–Bénard module (problem = 'RBC') solves the Boussinesq
equations: incompressible flow driven by a buoyancy force arising from
temperature-dependent density variations, with the temperature field T now
coupled back into the momentum equation.

    div(u) = 0

    d(u)/dt + (u . grad)u = -grad(p) + sqrt(Pr/Ra) * laplacian(u) + T * z_hat

    d(T)/dt + (u . grad)T = (1/sqrt(Ra*Pr)) * laplacian(T)

Here z_hat is the unit vector opposing gravity (buoyancy acts vertically),
and the Boussinesq approximation treats density variations as negligible
except in this buoyancy term.

### 3.2 Non-Dimensionalization and Governing Parameters

Non-dimensionalization uses the layer depth as the length scale and a
free-fall/diffusive time scale, giving two controlling dimensionless groups:

| Parameter | Meaning                                                              |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Ra        | Rayleigh number — ratio of buoyancy driving to dissipative effects     |
| Pr        | Prandtl number — ratio of momentum to thermal diffusivity              |

### 3.3 Boundary Conditions and Onset of Convection

The classic configuration imposes fixed temperatures at the top and bottom
boundaries (hot below, cold above) with no-slip or free-slip velocity
conditions, and periodic conditions in the horizontal direction(s). Linear
stability theory predicts a critical Rayleigh number Ra_c (order 10^3,
depending on boundary conditions) below which the conductive state is
stable and above which convective rolls/cells develop; Ra in para.py should
be set relative to this threshold to select conductive vs. convective
regimes.

### 3.4 Closure Relations

- Pressure is obtained via the same projection/Poisson approach as the
  convection module (Chapter 1).
- The buoyancy term couples momentum and temperature directly; no separate
  equation of state is needed since density variation is linearized in T
  under the Boussinesq approximation.

## Chapter 4. Theory — Compressible Module

### 4.1 Governing Equations

The compressible module (problem = 'hydro') solves the compressible
Navier–Stokes equations in non-dimensional form. With state vector
q = (rho, rho*u, rho*v, E):

    d(rho)/dt + div(rho * u) = 0

    d(rho*u)/dt + div(rho * u ⊗ u + p*I) = div(tau) + f

    d(E)/dt + div[(E + p) * u] = div(tau . u) + div(K * grad(T))

Dropping the viscous terms recovers the Euler equations (problem = 'euler'),
on which the hydro solver is built.

### 4.2 Closure Relations

- Equation of state:
    p = (gamma - 1) * (E - 0.5 * rho * |u|^2)
    p = R_gas * rho * T,  with R_gas = 1 / (gamma * Ma^2)
- Viscous stress (Newtonian):
    tau_xx = mu * ((4/3)*d(u)/dx - (2/3)*d(v)/dy)
    tau_xy = mu * (d(u)/dy + d(v)/dx)
- Transport coefficients:
    mu = 1 / Re
    K = 1 / (Ma^2 * (gamma - 1) * Re * Pr)
- f is an optional forcing term (forcing = True).

### 4.3 Non-Dimensionalization and Governing Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning                                     |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------|
| gamma     | Ratio of specific heats                        |
| Ma        | Mach number                                    |
| Re        | Reynolds number                                |
| Pr        | Prandtl number (implicit in K, above)          |
